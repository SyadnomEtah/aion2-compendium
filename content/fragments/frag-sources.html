Sources and how much to trust them

AION 2 has been live in Korea and Taiwan since November 2025, but global does not launch until October 5, 2026.

Almost nothing in this guide comes from an official global source. Most of it comes from community guides describing the KR/TW client, translated and re-translated by people who sell gold and boosting on the side.

This panel explains the badges you will see everywhere else in the guide, lists every site that fed this research and how much to trust it, and lays out everything that is still genuinely unknown going into launch.

How to read the badges

legend

Each badge shows how much agreement backs a claim, not how important that claim is.

Every fact-bearing card, bullet, or table row in this guide carries one of these badges next to it. They describe how much agreement exists behind a claim, not how important the claim is.

  • confirmed Two or more independent sources agree on this. Still not official, but it is not one guide's guess either.
  • single source Only one write-up documents this. Could be right, could be a translation slip or a one-off patch quirk. Treat as a starting point, not gospel.
  • contested Two or more sources give different numbers, names, or rankings for the same thing. Both versions are usually shown so you can judge for yourself.
  • KR-era This number or mechanic comes from the Korean or Taiwanese live client. Global launches with different tuning, sometimes very different (see the monetization walk-back after the KR launch backlash), so treat KR-era numbers as directional, not final.
  • official Traced back to NCSoft, PLAYNC, the NC newsroom, or an official Steam/store listing rather than a fan translation.
  • marketing Comes from a boosting, carry, or gold-selling site.

    These sites write a lot of the detailed guide content in this space (there simply isn't much else pre-launch), so their factual claims are used when nothing better exists, but any framing that nudges toward "buy this service" is not neutral advice.

Beginner tip: if a card only carries single source or marketing badges, read it as "plausible, worth trying, don't panic if the live game disagrees."

Source trust tiers

reference

Sources range from official NCSoft releases down to boosting-site guesswork; weigh each claim by its tier.

Every site cited anywhere in this guide falls into one of five buckets. The full row-by-row list is in the Master source list card below; this card explains what each bucket means and flags anything notable that happened when a page was fetched for this research (September 12, 2026).

Official

  • nc.com (NC newsroom): NCSoft's own press releases. Highest trust available, used for the KR/TW launch date. nc.com, Nov 2025
  • gamespress.com: an official press-release syndication feed, used for the Founder's Pack early-access announcement. gamespress.com
  • store.steampowered.com: the official Steam store listing for the Founder's Pack. store.steampowered.com

    Its content could not actually be scraped for this research (age-gate blocked the automated fetch), so its contents are corroborated only through four secondary sources, not read directly.

  • aion2.plaync.com and a Steam community-hub page: neither was directly fetched at all.

    Anything attributed to "official" announcements (the gamescom reveal, the July 8 2026 monetization plan) actually comes from secondary community coverage of those announcements, not a direct read.

Community databases and wikis

  • aion2hub.com: the single most-cited site in this guide, a fan-run database and guide hub with structured class, item, achievement, and patch-note pages. Treated as moderate-to-reasonably-reliable.

    Used throughout this guide. Its own "patch notes" pages are fan translations of the KR client, not official NC text.

  • aion2.wiki.fextralife.com (Fextralife): community wiki, used heavily for class overviews and the Daevanion board structure. Several class pages are explicitly marked by Fextralife itself as translated and in-progress, subject to change.
  • aion2wiki.com, aion2kina.com, aion2.online, aion2base.com, thegameswiki.com, kodex.yavuz.app, talentbuilds.com: smaller fan wikis, checklists, and planner tools. Generally treated as moderate trust for mechanics.

    Numeric specifics (energy caps, daily/weekly counts) are frequently single-source and KR-era. thegameswiki.com's own checklist pages returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch for some queries.

  • aion.fandom.com: this is the original 2009 AION's wiki, not AION 2.

    It surfaces in search results for stigma and class terminology and was explicitly excluded from claims except where flagged as legacy/uncertain relevance (see the Templar and Chanter class sections).

  • inven.co.kr: a major Korean player forum. A handful of specific threads were reached and cited directly (Gladiator and Assassin combo discussions).

    Most other Korean-language threads referenced in the research (inven, dcinside, fmkorea, arca.live, namu.wiki) returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch and are relayed only through search-engine snippets, the lowest-confidence sourcing tier used anywhere in this guide.

  • namu.wiki: a large Korean community wiki, referenced as a likely source of untranslated mechanics detail.

    Returned HTTP 403 on every direct fetch attempt in this research; nothing here is sourced to namu.wiki directly, only to English write-ups that may draw on it.

  • reddit: no r/aion2 threads were successfully indexed or fetched.

    Where "Reddit" appears in a citation (for example the Founder's Pack value debate), it is actually a Steam Community discussion thread cited via search summary, not a Reddit post.

    One citation in the Templar section literally links to google.com instead of an actual Reddit thread; it is not a real source, and is kept in the master list below and flagged as a broken link so nothing is silently dropped.

  • steamcommunity.com: player discussion threads, used for player sentiment on Founder's Pack value and pay-to-win concerns. Treated as anecdotal opinion, not fact.
  • game.savetip.co.kr, redwendy.com, differ.blog, daevasreport.com, 6sword.com, aion-nova.com, aion2-assassin-guide.onrender.com, questlog.gg: smaller Korean-language blogs, fan-made build sites, and patch-note trackers.

    The savetip.co.kr Od Energy article is the single lowest-confidence numeric source used in the Dungeons section (403 on direct fetch, relayed via search snippet only, yet it is the only source for the "1,400 energy per week" figure).

Guide and news sites

  • mmorpg.com, massivelyop.com, mein-mmo.de, mmoculture.com, mmohuts.com, thegamer.com, escapistmagazine.com, vpesports.com, gamemeca.com: mainstream or long-running enthusiast gaming press.

    Used for the KR launch controversy and the official monetization plan coverage. Two massivelyop.com articles (the Nov 2025 KR-backlash piece and, indirectly, coverage of the July 2026 plan) returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch and are relayed via search-engine summaries rather than a full read.

  • timesaver.gg, slashingcreeps.com, ldcloud.net, exitlag.com, lagofast.com, noping.com: boosting-adjacent guide sites and network-tool (VPN/ping) company blogs that publish detailed AION 2 guides as content marketing.

    exitlag.com in particular returned HTTP 403 on a large fraction of the pages cited across this research (guilds, professions, open world, PvP, dungeons, class builds); those claims rely on search-result summaries only.

  • allthings.how: cites leaderboard data rather than pure opinion for its PvE tier list, making it more trustworthy than most tier lists here, though it is still a single source.
  • gamesolusi.com, medium.com, keyran.net, valksintavsiyesi.com, ggwtb.com, lootwow.com, oslink.io, theprofanedgeek.com, mobilematters.gg, iggm.com: independent blogs, aggregator sites, and codes-roundup pages.

    valksintavsiyesi.com returned 403 Forbidden on direct fetch; medium.com's Ranger macro post also returned 403.

Boosting and marketing sites

  • overgear.com, leprestore.com, mmoexp.com, u4n.com, u4gm.com, boostroom.com, pvpbank.com, expcarry.com, aion2boost.com, vortexgaming.io, aoeah.com, ssegold.com, rsnxt.com, mmom.com, boosting-ground.com: gold-selling, power-leveling, and carry-service sites.

    This bucket supplied the largest single share of citations in this research (over a third of all unique URLs), simply because there is little else written about AION 2's build mechanics pre-launch. Their factual claims about mechanics are used when corroborated or when nothing better exists; their framing toward "buy our service" is not.

  • Several of these sites returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch across the research.

    Overgear.com, leprestore.com, exitlag-adjacent boosting pages, u4gm.com's Assassin and Templar build pages, and boosting-ground.com are affected; their content is relayed via search-engine snippets rather than a verified full read.

  • vortexgaming.io: the single most-cited boosting-adjacent site for class build detail (skills, stigmas, Arcana, macros) across nearly every class section.

    Its guides are versioned to specific KR/TW patches. Treat its numbers as a snapshot of one patch, not a permanent target.

Video sources

  • YouTube: eleven videos are cited across this research, mostly for their title and description only.

    Transcripts and full content generally could not be retrieved; where a video's actual content was not verified, the relevant section says so explicitly (see Ranger and Sorcerer macro-setup videos).

Avoid: treating any single boosting-site guide, or any pre-launch tier list, as a settled answer. Balance patches between now and October 5, 2026, and again after global launch, can and likely will change specific numbers.

Research method

This guide is built from search and page fetches, not hands-on play, and treats KR/TW numbers as directional, not final.

This guide was compiled on September 12, 2026, roughly three weeks before global launch, using web search and page fetches rather than hands-on play, since the global client does not exist yet.

Topics were researched separately: the general overview, systems, monetization, and dungeons/energy topics, and one per class. Every claim is linked inline and badged by confidence.

Method

  • Search for recent AION 2 coverage on the target topic.
  • Fetch the most promising pages directly.
  • Fall back to search-engine result snippets when a fetch failed (a large number did, mostly HTTP 403 from boosting and VPN-marketing sites that block automated readers).

Every claim was tagged at the point of writing as confirmed, single source, or contested, based on how many independent sources agreed. It was separately flagged KR-era whenever a number could only be traced to the Korean or Taiwanese live client rather than an official global statement.

The KR/TW-versus-global gap

The single biggest limitation running through this entire guide is the KR/TW-versus-global gap. AION 2 has been live in Korea and Taiwan since November 2025, so almost every specific number here, from skill breakpoints to dungeon entry limits to Stigma slot counts, was measured on that client.

Global launches October 5, 2026 with its own balance pass (NC has already announced changes like no Heroic-gear drops and different item-level gates), so treat every KR-era number as a reasonable starting guess, not a locked-in fact.

No fact in this guide was invented to fill a gap. Where research genuinely turned up nothing, the relevant card says "no reliable source found" rather than guessing, and those gaps are collected in the Known unknowns card above so they are easy to re-check once Founder access opens on September 30, 2026 and the client is actually in players' hands.

Citation reliability: a random sample of 20 sourced sentences was re-checked against the linked pages; 15 were fully supported, 1 partially (correct skill names, grouping added by this guide), and 3 pointed at a page that did not carry the claim. Assume a residual error rate of roughly one in seven chips: click through before acting on any specific number.
